Two seconds early. Or worse, drifting.

You know the problem. You download subtitles for a film, and they're two seconds early. Or they drift: fine at the start, unusable by minute 40. Or it's a Japanese film and the subtitles you found were made for another release.

The original SubSync by Michał Szymaniak solved this for many languages, but it's no longer maintained and couldn't handle Japanese films or subtitles in another language than the audio. SubSync Echo continues it and adds Whisper speech recognition and translation, all running on your own computer.

No account. No ads. Your videos and subtitles are never uploaded. Just a tool that listens to your video and fixes your subtitles.

Under 0.2 seconds off isn't noticeable when watching. That's the bar every test is measured against.

Every line of Tears of Steel, in all 6 languages, came out within it. All results

What makes it different from shifting the timing by hand.

It actually listens.

Instead of you nudging an offset until it looks right, it transcribes parts of the film with Whisper, matches the words with your subtitles, and fits every line to the real speech, including subtitles that drift because they were made for another frame rate.

Subtitles don't need to match the audio language.

Spanish subtitles on an English film. English subtitles on a Japanese anime. It translates on your computer, with Whisper and Meta's M2M100, matches the words and syncs anyway.

Nothing leaves your computer.

Speech recognition runs locally. Translation runs locally. The only downloads are the models it needs, once: the speech model (about 490 MB), plus the translation model (another 490 MB) for subtitles that aren't in English. After that it works offline, and your films and subtitles don't go anywhere.

No graphics card. No fancy hardware.

It runs on the processor. The 44 seconds above were measured on a laptop with an AMD Ryzen 9 5900HX and 8 GB of RAM, no graphics card. A slower machine takes longer, but it works.

It saves nothing rather than something wrong.

Before saving, the timing is checked against the speech in the whole film. If it can't be confirmed, it tells you and saves no file. Your subtitle text and styling stay exactly as they were; only the times change.

It can watch a folder for you. Coming with 0.21

Watch mode syncs each new subtitle that appears next to its video in your Jellyfin, Plex or Emby library. Bazarr can run it after every download, and a Docker image runs it on your home server. Home server guide

Will it actually help you?

Let's not waste your time. Here's the honest breakdown.

It'll help if…

  • Your subtitles are early or late all the way through
  • They drift: fine at first, worse as the film goes on
  • They were made for a different release or frame rate
  • It's anime or a foreign film, and the subtitles are in another language

It won't if…

  • You don't have subtitles yet: it fixes timing, it doesn't write them
  • The subtitles are burned into the picture
  • You're on a Mac: it runs on Windows and Linux
  • Your computer has less than 8 GB of RAM

Things people ask.

More in the user guide, but here are the short answers.

Is it actually free?
Yes. GPL-3.0, no account, no ads, no "pro" tier. The source is on GitHub and the downloads are free.
Are my videos or subtitles uploaded anywhere?
No. Speech recognition and translation run on your computer. The only downloads are the models it needs, once, always the exact versions SubSync Echo was tested with. More about privacy
Do I need a graphics card?
No. It runs on the processor. The times on this page came from a laptop with an AMD Ryzen 9 5900HX and 8 GB of RAM, no graphics card. A slower processor just takes longer.
Can it run automatically when new subtitles are downloaded?
Yes, from 0.21: watch mode syncs each new subtitle placed next to its video, Bazarr can run it after each download, and there's a Docker image for home servers. A build for ARM computers such as the Raspberry Pi is planned, not built yet.
Is there a Mac version?
No. It runs on Windows and Linux.
How do you know the sync is accurate?
Every number comes from a real test: correct subtitles are mistimed on purpose, synced, and compared line by line with the right timing. Japanese films with English subtitles: every line within 0.2 s in 7 of 9 films. Tears of Steel: all 6 languages. Sita Sings the Blues: 4 of 5 languages, songs included. The full results are in doc/results.md, and you can run the same tests yourself with one command.

Give it a try.

Download it, point it at a mistimed subtitle, and click Start. If it doesn't work for you, tell us on GitHub. That's how it gets better.
